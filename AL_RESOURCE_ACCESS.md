# AL resource ownership and authorization plan

This document is an inspection and a proposal, not an implemented access policy. It uses [AL_DATABASE_SCHEMA.md](AL_DATABASE_SCHEMA.md), [AL_PERMISSIONS.md](AL_PERMISSIONS.md), the current controllers, and read-only Oracle aggregate queries. Counts below are a snapshot, not authorization rules. No account details are included.

## Interpretation and baseline

- A foreign key proves a database association, not a business ownership, tenancy, confidentiality, or cross-organization sharing rule.
- Authenticated membership is currently obtained from `USERS.COMPANY_ID` joined to `COMPANIES.COMPANY_ID`; the linked company's `COMPANY_TYPE`, not the user's declared type or role, is the verified type. Organization membership and the provisional role permissions in `AL_PERMISSIONS.md` are separate checks.
- **Restrict unassigned** means omit rows with null, missing, ambiguous, or unresolved ownership from organization-specific access. An explicitly approved, separately authorized remediation workflow may review them later; it is not implied here.
- All proposed reads and management below are subject to authentication, verified organization membership, approved role permissions, and server-side resource-scope checks. Management remains disabled until SQL alignment, authorization, concurrency, and CSRF safeguards are implemented. No proposal grants global access merely because a user is Admin.
- Current policy remains unchanged: product, product-image, and supplier-directory GETs are global marketplace reads for authenticated users with verified organizations; operational GETs require the explicit development allowlist; users/profiles are restricted; all business mutations are disabled. These present global marketplace endpoints are **not** evidence that their data is private or company-scoped.

## Verified relationships and important limits

1. `USERS.COMPANY_ID → COMPANIES.COMPANY_ID`; `USERS.ROLE_ID → ROLES.ROLE_ID`. `COMPANIES.USER_ID → USERS.USER_ID` is a separate FK and must not be substituted for user membership or interpreted as exclusive company ownership without a rule.
2. `PRODUCTS.COMPANY_ID → COMPANIES`; `PRODUCTS.WAREHOUSE_ID → WAREHOUSES`; `PRODUCT_IMAGES.PRODUCT_ID`, `STOCK_ADJUSTMENTS.PRODUCT_ID`, `PRODUCTIONS.PRODUCT_ID`, and `INVENTORY_MOVEMENTS.PRODUCT_ID` reference products.
3. `SUPPLIER_ORDERS` references company, user, product, and status. No active supplier-party FK establishes supplier-order ownership. `PAYMENTS` and `INVOICES` reference supplier orders; inventory movements may also reference an order.
4. `PRODUCTIONS.RECIPE_ID → PRODUCTION_RECIPES`; recipes have no company reference. `USER_PROFILES.USER_ID → USERS`; free-text names are not identity keys.
5. All relevant company or intermediary references above are nullable except `INVOICES.SUPPLIER_ORDER_ID`. The documented FK paths validate referential links when populated, not their business interpretation.

## Filtering readiness and restrictions

- **Safest first candidates:** a user's own identity (`/auth/me`, already implemented), then a narrowly scoped same-company user directory after privacy approval. `USERS.COMPANY_ID` supports the current membership association; it does not by itself authorize profile data or user administration.
- **Conditional candidates:** associated products by `PRODUCTS.COMPANY_ID`, and image visibility inherited from product, after confirming catalog ownership and marketplace publication rules. The sparse links mean such filters would exclude many existing records; never silently assign them.
- **Not yet safe:** warehouses, supplier orders, inventory movements, payments, invoices, productions, recipes, and stock adjustments. Foreign-key joins can locate associated companies in some rows, but do not establish the relevant owner or participating party. User profiles remain self/privacy-policy dependent.
- The current marketplace endpoints intentionally return a shared global directory/catalog under the temporary policy; their future visibility model needs a decision. Organization filtering must not be introduced as a security claim while these global reads remain exposed.
- List and detail endpoints must enforce equivalent scope server-side, including joins, counts, aggregations, exports, related-resource reads, and not-found/forbidden behavior. Never trust a client-provided company ID as the authenticated scope.

## Decisions before implementation

1. Does `PRODUCTS.COMPANY_ID` represent product owner, publisher, producer, or seller? Which fields and images are publicly shared in the B2B marketplace?
2. What does `SUPPLIER_ORDERS.COMPANY_ID` represent? How are buyer and supplier/fulfiller identified, and what may each see and do?
3. Who owns or operates warehouses, inventory movements, production, recipes, and adjustments? Are facilities, recipes, or stock shared across organizations?
4. Who are the payer/payee and invoice issuer/recipient, and which payment and invoice fields may each party read?
5. What user-directory and profile fields are visible to colleagues, administrators, and the user themself? How should multiple profiles and potential multi-company membership work?
6. What should happen to null/legacy associations, and who may review them? Do not reassign them automatically.
7. Approve or revise the provisional role matrix in `AL_PERMISSIONS.md`, including supplier-specific access, before enabling permission checks or management actions.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Approve membership, marketplace visibility, ownership semantics, and role/privacy policies; inventory unresolved legacy associations without changing them.
2. Design and test reusable authenticated-identity, organization-scope, and permission checks; retain the existing development allowlist until an approved replacement exists.
3. Implement and test a minimal self-only identity/profile flow and, if approved, same-company user-directory reads with explicit field selection.
4. Once product association and publication semantics are approved, implement separate scoped product/image management reads and explicit marketplace visibility. Cover null links, cross-company IDs, detail/list equivalence, and indirect images.
5. Establish explicit order parties and warehouse/operator relationships before implementing order, warehouse, and inventory filtering. Then define scoped production, recipes, and stock-adjustment access.
6. Define payer/payee and issuer/recipient access before payment or invoice filtering. Keep financial data restricted until then.
7. Only after ownership, permissions, SQL validation, and CSRF controls are complete, consider re-enabling individual mutations with tests proving cross-organization denial and no mass updates.
